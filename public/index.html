Who we are

Transparent giving, trusted selection

Empower Nyamagabe is a web platform built to support vulnerable families in Nyamagabe District with the two costs that most often push children out of school and families out of health cover: school fees and Community Based Health Insurance (Mutuelle de Santé).

Most donation platforms solve only one half of the trust problem. Some show where money goes but not who was chosen; informal giving is fast but leaves no record. We combine both: families are confirmed by Umudugudu and Ubudehe leaders who know them, and every payment is recorded against a specific family and need.

Empower Nyamagabe is a capstone research pilot. It is being tested with donors, families and local leaders in one sector of the district.

Two kinds of trust, one platform

Donors trust who receives help because the community chose them, and trust where their money went because every payment is traceable.

How it works

From verification to a traceable gift

Four steps connect a family’s need to a donor’s gift.

1. A local leader submits a family

An Umudugudu or Ubudehe committee member records the household’s Ubudehe category and its need, with supporting notes.

2. The family is verified

A leader or the administrator approves or rejects the submission. Only approved families ever appear to donors.

3. A donor gives through Flutterwave

Pay with Mobile Money or card on Flutterwave’s secure page. This platform never sees or stores your payment details.

4. See exactly where it went

Each donation is recorded against a family code and a specific need, with its Flutterwave reference, in your donation history.

Why this matters

One problem, two symptoms

Nyamagabe District has a poverty rate of 51.4%, almost double the national rate of 27.4% (NISR, EICV7). That poverty shows up in two connected ways: children leave school, and families struggle to keep paying for Mutuelle de Santé.

A family that cannot cover a health emergency and school costs at the same time is forced to choose. Research in Rwanda links health insurance to fewer children dropping out of school.

  • School dropout Dropout is 7% in primary, 11% in lower secondary and 6% in upper secondary school. Primary repetition is 32%, against 21% nationally.
  • Health cover gap Coverage rose from 75.2% (2017) to 96.1% (2024), but the remaining 3.9% still means about 14,500 people without insurance.
  • A trust gap in giving International platforms need a bank account or card, and cash giving leaves no record. Donors need to trust both who is helped and where money goes.

Sources: NISR EICV7 Poverty Profile (2024); Nyamagabe District Development Strategy 2024–2029.

How we stay accountable

Built to be checked

Accountability is part of the design, not an add-on.

Community verification

Families are built on Rwanda’s Ubudehe classification and confirmed by local leaders. Rejections need a written reason, and every decision is logged.

Traceable donations

Each gift is linked to one family and one need, with its Flutterwave reference and payment status.

Payment details stay with Flutterwave

Mobile Money numbers, PINs and card numbers are entered only on Flutterwave’s page. We store the reference, amount, status, family and purpose.

Dignity and privacy

Donors never see a family’s name, phone number or village. Only the need and general location are shown.

Verification guide

How decisions are made on this platform.

Empower Nyamagabe builds on the Ubudehe classification and your knowledge of households in your village. The platform adds a record of who submitted and who decided, so that decisions can be reviewed.

  • Check the household is in a lower Ubudehe category and has a real school-fee or Mutuelle de Santé need.
  • Write the public description without identifying details. Do not include names, phone numbers or the village. Donors see this text.
  • Give a reason when rejecting. It is stored in the audit trail.
  • Participation is voluntary. Taking part in the pilot never changes whether a family is chosen to receive help.
